## Research question and scope

The study asks whether independently trained Go engines exhibit related representational blind spots around cyclic adversarial positions. Distinguish distribution shift, information accessible to a probe, behavioral use, and causal contribution. Accessible information alone does not establish policy reliance or a shared failure mechanism.

Start with `BUILD_STATUS.md`, the earlier milestone status files, and `docs/research-design.md`.

## Data and extraction validation — milestone 2

The aligned dataset has 60 ordinary self-play games / 688 positions, plus nine public attack-source games / 98 positions. Ordinary train/validation/test partitions have 36/12/12 games and 410/133/145 positions. No exact or symmetric boards cross splits in this sample.

Ordinary games are the first 60 complete trees in an official-archive mirror, generated by LZ #270. Extraction uses LZ #57 and KataGo. This is a convenience replay cohort, not an on-policy evaluation of the extracted engines. Attack-source membership does not label attack onset or reproduced failure. The pinned compressed prefix and per-SGF hashes are verified; the entire remote archive hash and complete XZ checksum are not.

Leela v1/v2 extraction was compared with instrumented native CPU inference on 12 selected public/capture/pass inputs. The milestone-2 report records maximum policy/value differences around 1.94e-6 / 2.03e-6 and checked trunk difference 2.41e-5. These are bounded checkpoint/backend/fixture checks. Canonical arrays use top-origin [row,column], native Leela uses bottom-origin coordinates; preserve current-player perspective and newest-first 18-plane history encoding.

KataGo trained extraction IS completed through the native route. Reference and tensor-capture builds produced identical printed outputs on 26 empty/ordinary/attack/capture/pass states; reconstructed policy/value/ownership errors were 5.13e-7 / 5.08e-7 / 8.32e-8. Input board/player/liberty planes were independently checked at all 786 positions. This is bounded export/output validation, not an independent trunk implementation or universal bitwise proof. The separate experimental Python bridge's untrained fixture must not be confused with the trained native extraction.

## Controlled exploratory decoding — milestone 3

Completed: 69 probe fits, 26 reported conditions, five-seed nonlinear/random/shuffled controls, 34,066 fixed chain anchors, and an independent saved-prediction/artifact audit. Historical test evidence records 105 passing tests.

Final-layer linear liberty-classification accuracy:
| Engine | Ordinary | Attack-source | Change and 95% interval |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Leela Zero #57 | 45.9% | 46.2% | +0.3 percentage points [-3.6, +3.9] |
| KataGo b6c96 | 60.4% | 50.3% | -10.1 points [-14.5, -5.6] |

The pilot does not show the same final-layer linear decline in both engines. These are probe accuracies, not win rates. KataGo directly supplies liberty features; its spatial-input control perfectly decodes supplied 1/2/3/4+ bins. Random-network/cohort effects and nonlinear readout differences prevent interpreting the drop as a learned shared mechanism. Fixed-stratum matching reduces KataGo's descriptive gap to -4.6 points, but 1,999/2,000 bootstrap draws lose required strata, so no matched interval is asserted. This exploratory matching is not the separately reserved matched ordinary outcome cohort requested next.

## Bounded native behavioral findings — milestone 4

Selected 24 named-victim positions from six existing public attack games before search, following inventory of nine games / 11 large captures / 44 candidates. Completed 48 discovery searches plus 15 additional sensitivity searches, each using a fresh native process and full SGF prefix. Discovery used 128 root visits and seed 11.

| Engine | Discovery positions | Avoidable immediate target-chain loss | No immediate defense |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Leela Zero #57 | 24 | 3 | 6 |
| KataGo b6c96 s175M | 24 | 1 | 6 |

The certificate exhaustively tests legal defenses and next replies while keeping original target stones fixed. It establishes that the selected move permits immediate capture although a legal alternative preserves all original target stones against every legal next reply.

Concrete examples:
- Game 1, decision 249: Leela chose N12; R9 captures 49 original target stones. Playing R9 instead supplies a one-reply defense.
- Game 6, decision 221: Leela chose K1 and KataGo chose J11; A8 captures 34 original target stones and is also the immediate defense.

Four discovery cases have annotated SGFs and figures. Three selected cases meet the local criterion at 128 and 512 visits for seeds 11, 22 and 33. Those 18 selected-case observations include three reused discovery searches; repeats are sensitivity evidence selected on discovery outcomes, not independent confirmation rates. There are 19 positive observations overall, with native branch checks. Independent audits checked 63 chosen moves and 8,993 legal immediate replies. Historical milestone evidence reports 155 distinct tests across regression, legality and optional pinned-source checks; tests were not rerun for this Commons entry.

Boundaries:
- Immediate material loss does not establish a globally bad sacrifice, eventual chain death, avoidable whole-game loss, superhuman weakness, or causal representation failure.
- Counts are development observations in retrospectively capture-selected, overlapping positions, not population failure rates.
- The same source games were already used in the decoding pilot; do not reuse them as a fresh reserved cohort.
- Historical engineering checkpoints are not certified original attack victims; victim weights remain unidentified. This is not a reproduction of the published 4096-visit configuration.
- Victim colors alternate; game 8's victim is White. Game 9 records a victim win despite ring capture.
- GTP play acceptance alone is not legality evidence. Keep independent board transitions, simple-ko/no-suicide checks and engine-specific repetition checks.
- Non-failures do not show an attack is solved; cases lacking a defensive alternative cannot establish avoidability.
